# Governance

Version 0.1.0 — adopted 2026-09-17 UTC.

## Founding steward

**PEER TO PEER PRIVACY SYSTEMS RESEARCH LLC**, an Oregon limited liability company, is the founding member and initial steward of TPNIL. It holds governance authority during establishment and is the Steward identified in the laboratory's contribution and artifact-submission agreements.

The steward maintains an impartial, merit-led laboratory under the [charter](CHARTER.md). It appoints initial maintainers, preserves records, manages operational resources, and enforces published criteria. Technical requirements apply equally to its own software and all other SUTs. Neither the steward nor a sponsor may override failed assertions or suppress unfavorable admitted results.

## Decisions and transparency

The repository records governance decisions, delegations, affiliations, conflicts, recusals, votes, dissents, interpretations, grants of authority, and changes of stewardship. Records identify the responsible participants, rationale, supporting evidence, and affected requirements.

The repository is private during establishment. Public release requires a recorded decision by the current steward under the governance doctrine and includes the governance decision history.

## Governance vision

TPNIL aims to become a durable, meritocratic institution governed through rough consensus, demonstrated competence, and accountable stewardship. The Internet and Linux ecosystems are leading examples for this vision: open technical deliberation, working implementations, sustained contribution, and trusted maintainers can support infrastructure across organizations and generations.

Technical decisions seek rough consensus through substantive consideration of objections and evidence. Maintainer responsibility is earned through useful work, sound judgment, review, and service to the community. The Internet's [rough-consensus practice](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7282.html) and Linux's [maintainer-led development](https://docs.kernel.org/process/maintainer-handbooks.html) inform these aims. Formal votes provide accountable institutional decisions; vote totals alone do not establish technical consensus or override conformance evidence.

This vision applies to every prospective stewardship path: a participant council, BSV Association, or another agreed institutional arrangement. Each path must preserve equal participation, technical independence, open deliberation, earned responsibility, and continuity of the laboratory's records and grants.

### Affiliation and voting rules

An organization and all participating persons under its employment or common control form one voting group. Each independent group has at most one council vote, regardless of its number of participants. Accordingly, P2PPSR and Ty share one vote; later acceptance by Brayden or Tone adds participation within that same group. The same rule groups BSVA with Darren, Siggi, Connor, and Dylan, and Binary Labs with Mitch, as those parties accept.

Each group records a shared position or an authorized voting delegate. Without an agreed position or delegation, the group abstains. A person's organizational representation does not create a second individual membership unless that person separately accepts in an individual capacity. Every accepted person or entity is counted once toward the participation threshold; affiliation groups, rather than individual memberships, determine voting weight and voting quorum.

Affiliations and conflicts are confirmed before activation or voting and updated when they change. A pending disclosure is ineligible for an independence count. Common control and overlapping affiliations are resolved in the recorded grouping before votes are counted, so one controlling interest cannot obtain multiple votes. An unaffiliated individual with completed disclosures may form an independent group. Recusal applies to the whole group when its shared institutional interest is implicated; otherwise the vote requires an unconflicted authorized representative.

## Roles and independence

- **Steward:** legal and operational custody, access, finances, archives, and succession.
- **Maintainers and program editors:** technical proposals, versioned requirements, infrastructure, and coverage.
- **Operators:** artifact intake, isolated execution, observation, and evidence preservation.
- **Independent reviewers:** evaluation and reproduction without conflicts in the subject result.
- **Appeals panel:** unconflicted participants outside the original decision.

Maintainer responsibility is earned through sustained contribution, review quality, competence, reliability, and conduct. Sponsorship and fees are disclosed and governed by uniform terms. Payment has no bearing on verdicts. Contributors are recused from adjudicating their own disputed certification results.

## Provisional stewardship paths

The laboratory keeps several paths open toward collaborative multi-stakeholder governance. These are prospective options, with the council activation mechanism below already authorized by this doctrine. P2PPSR retains stewardship until a transition takes effect.

### Standing council path

The founding steward makes and records decisions during establishment. Technical adoption should receive two unconflicted reviews when available. Work adopted with steward review alone retains experimental status.

The participant council activates when:

1. At least five distinct participants have expressly accepted, with completed disclosures establishing at least three independent affiliation groups.
2. A transition charter has received at least 30 days of review. It implements this doctrine, preserves participation for later contributors, and specifies maintainer succession, finances, appeals, and operational custody.
3. A recorded activation resolution includes the accepted participant register, affiliation groups, authorized delegates, transition charter, initial responsibilities, and effective date. At least five accepted participants spanning at least three independent groups assent to that resolution; approval also requires two thirds of eligible affiliation-group votes.

This doctrine is P2PPSR's standing authorization for that transition. When these conditions are met, the council assumes laboratory governance on the recorded effective date, without a further discretionary approval or veto by P2PPSR. Activation may occur before the first independently reproduced integrated profile. P2PPSR shall facilitate the transfer of administrative access, records, and operational responsibilities.

Governance authority transfers upon activation. Legal title, contracts, and custodial obligations transfer through the instruments and consents they require. Until those transfers are complete, P2PPSR holds the affected assets and obligations in transitional custody, implements lawful council decisions, and records remaining transfer requirements. Existing grants and preservation duties continue throughout the transition.

After activation, quorum requires representation of a majority of active accepted affiliation groups, including at least three unconflicted groups. Each represented group has one vote. Ordinary institutional motions require a majority of unconflicted votes cast. Charter, licensing-policy, and custody changes require two thirds of unconflicted votes cast and a recorded rights-impact review. Abstentions are excluded from votes cast. Recusals that remove quorum defer adjudication until unconflicted participation is available. Technical deliberation continues through the rough-consensus process described above.

### Other arrangements and transition review

Other institutional arrangements may be considered when they advance the same meritocratic, rough-consensus, multi-stakeholder vision. A proposed transition receives at least 30 days of review and records the accepting parties, authority, responsibilities, and effective date.

Within 90 days after the first independently reproduced integrated profile, the current steward shall publish a transition review covering representation, maintainer succession, proposals, working groups, finances, appeals, and key and archive custody. This milestone ensures a review of progress; it is not a prerequisite for council activation. Outstanding participation or transfer conditions are recorded with the next steps toward resolution.

## Changes, disputes and appeals

Normative proposals include motivation, pinned references, requirement and vector changes, compatibility analysis, and evidence. Review periods are 14 calendar days for ordinary normative changes and 30 days for charter, licensing, or certification changes. Experimental exceptions require a recorded reason. An urgent defect may suspend a profile prospectively while preserving historical verdicts.

Anyone may challenge a result by identifying its digest, requirement, and evidence. The laboratory targets acknowledgment within seven days and disposition within 30, with delays and their reasons recorded. Disputed, corrected, and withdrawn statuses are appended to the original record.

Appeals are heard by reviewers independent of the original decision and subject implementation. A result lacking independent adjudication remains disputed or experimental and is ineligible for certification.

## Certification and continuity

Initial outputs are experimental. Certification requires an adopted mark scheme, at least two unconflicted reviewers, independent laboratory reproduction, a functioning appeals process, lawful artifact retention, signed checkpoints, and independently held mirrors.

Institutional operation requires at least two administrative custodians and a tested succession and archive-recovery procedure. A successor accepts preservation duties and existing grants in writing. Changes of steward preserve historical requirements, results, and licenses.
